Best CCNA Resources in 2026: Free and Paid, Compared

The Routerel team· Updated: November 9, 2026· 11 min read

You don't need ten subscriptions to pass the CCNA. You need one solid theory source, somewhere to configure devices, a steady supply of practice questions, and the official exam topics so you know when you're done.

Below are the resources we'd recommend, grouped by type. For each one you'll find what it is, what it costs, who it suits, and its honest strengths and weaknesses. Prices were checked on November 9, 2026 against the official sites (links in the Sources section at the end) and can change at any time.

One thing to keep in mind before you buy anything: Cisco moves the exam from version 1.1 to version 2.0 on February 3, 2027 (the exam code stays 200-301). If you'll test after that date, check which version each resource targets. The details are in our guide to the CCNA.

Official Cisco resources

The official exam topics

What it is: the document where Cisco lists everything that can appear on the exam, with the weight of each domain. There's a PDF for v1.1 and one for v2.0.

Cost: free.

Who it's for: everyone. It's the only source that officially defines the scope of the exam.

Pros: tells you exactly what to know, and works as a checklist.

Cons: explains nothing. It's a map, not a textbook.

Cisco Learning Network

What it is: Cisco's official certification community, with discussions, articles, online events and community-sourced study material for each certification.

Cost: the community resources are free; you'll want a Cisco account to take part in discussions.

Who it's for: self-learners who need somewhere to ask questions.

Pros: it's the official venue, full of people studying for the same exam.

Cons: post quality varies, and nothing is organized like a course.

Cisco U.

What it is: Cisco's online learning platform. There's a free tier (you sign in with your Cisco ID) with tutorials, webinars and other content, while the paid subscriptions include the full CCNA learning path with labs and assessments. Cisco's exam page also mentions a CCNA practice exam in Cisco U.

Cost: the free tier doesn't include the CCNA path. The path is part of the Essentials subscription, which a Cisco training partner lists at 1,800 USD per year (18 Cisco Learning Credits). Your own price shows up on the plans page once you sign in. At the end of May 2026, Cisco also announced no-cost early access to its updated official CCNA training on Cisco U.; check whether it's still available when you read this.

Who it's for: learners with a large budget or an employer paying for training.

Pros: official content aligned with the exam topics, labs included, and continuing education credits toward recertification.

Cons: expensive if you're paying out of pocket, and you only see the real price after signing in.

Networking Academy CCNA courses

What it is: Cisco's education program for schools, universities and training centers. The CCNA material is split into three courses: CCNA 1 · ITN (Introduction to Networks), CCNA 2 · IEN (Implementing Enterprise Networks) and CCNA 3 · CEN (Connecting Enterprise Networks), with modules, Packet Tracer labs and quizzes.

Cost: the CCNA courses are usually taken through an academy with an instructor, and the institution sets the price (many universities and high schools include it in their programs). Netacad.com also has free self-paced courses such as Networking Basics and Getting Started with Cisco Packet Tracer. Check each course page for the options available for the CCNA series.

Who it's for: students, or anyone with an academy nearby.

Pros: a clear structure, ready-made labs, and an instructor to answer questions.

Cons: the class sets the pace, and module quizzes aren't exam-style tests.

Books

The CCNA 200-301 Official Cert Guide (Cisco Press) is the book you'll see recommended most often. The second edition, from 2024, comes in two volumes written by Wendell Odom, with David Hucaby and Jason Gooley on Volume 2.

What's included: beyond the theory, the publisher lists the Pearson Test Prep software with more than 800 practice questions, flashcards, online exercises, Network Simulator Lite and more than 8 hours of video.

Cost: the two-volume print library lists at 79.99 USD on ciscopress.com, and the publisher discounts it often. eBook editions and book-plus-eBook bundles are also available.

Who it's for: people who learn well by reading and want one complete theory source.

Pros: very thorough, organized by the exam topics, with yearly digital updates when Cisco changes the blueprint.

Cons: long (two thick volumes) and dense. The second edition covers v1.1; if you're aiming for v2.0, check ciscopress.com for a new edition before buying.

A practical tip: after each chapter, close the book and explain what you learned out loud. Rereading makes you feel like you know it; only testing shows whether you do.

Lab tools

You only learn configuration by configuring. There's a detailed comparison in our article Packet Tracer vs GNS3 vs EVE-NG; here's the short version.

Cisco Packet Tracer

What it is: Cisco's network simulator, used throughout the Networking Academy courses. It runs on Windows, Ubuntu Linux and macOS.

Cost: free with a Networking Academy account, which you can create for free, for example by enrolling in Getting Started with Cisco Packet Tracer.

Who it's for: anyone preparing for the CCNA. It's the recommended starting point.

Pros: runs on an ordinary laptop; includes routers, switches, PCs, servers and wireless; simulation mode lets you follow packets step by step.

Cons: it's a simulator, not real IOS, so some commands and options are missing or behave slightly differently.

Cisco Modeling Labs Free

What it is: the free edition of Cisco's official network emulator. It runs real Cisco images (IOL, IOL-L2, ASAv) plus a few Linux machines.

Cost: free, no license needed; you need a Cisco account.

Who it's for: anyone who wants real IOS, legally, without hunting for images.

Pros: official images included; the same platform as the paid editions.

Cons: at most 5 nodes running at once (unmanaged switches don't count), and it needs a stronger PC plus a VM or bare-metal install.

GNS3

What it is: an open-source network emulator (GPL v3 license), widely used in the community.

Cost: the software is free. Cisco images aren't included: GNS3 states plainly that it can't provide them for legal reasons, so you have to obtain them yourself, legally, for example through a Cisco service contract. Read each image's license: the images that ship with Cisco Modeling Labs, for instance, are licensed for use inside CML only.

Who it's for: experienced users who want complex or multi-vendor topologies.

Pros: flexible, large community, integrates VMs and containers.

Cons: harder to set up, the image question, and it's resource-hungry.

EVE-NG

What it is: an emulation platform that runs as a server (a VM or dedicated hardware) with a web interface.

Cost: the Freemium edition is free, limited to 7 nodes per instance (plus unlimited VPCS). The Base license costs 150 EUR. The old Community edition reached end of support in June 2026. As with GNS3, you bring your own Cisco images.

Who it's for: mostly CCNP candidates or large labs after the CCNA.

Pros: works from the browser; suits large labs and multiple users (on paid licenses).

Cons: more than you need for the CCNA, and the free tier's limit is tight.

Practice exams

Boson ExSim-Max for Cisco 200-301 CCNA

What it is: a set of CCNA practice exams that explain why each answer is right or wrong, with references. It has a simulation mode that matches the real exam's timing, plus a study mode.

Cost: 99 USD for a one-year subscription with auto-renewal (you can cancel). It includes 5 practice exams with 370 questions in total.

Who it's for: anyone who wants to check, in the final weeks, whether they're ready.

Pros: detailed explanations, and the timed format gets you used to the pressure.

Cons: the product page doesn't say which exam version the questions target, so ask before buying if you're taking v2.0. Once you've seen the questions, second-pass scores don't tell you much.

Practice tests bundled with other resources

Pearson Test Prep comes with the Official Cert Guide, and Cisco U. has a practice exam. If you already own one of those, use its tests before buying more.

Flashcards and spaced repetition

Plenty of CCNA material simply has to be memorized: administrative distances, port numbers, private address ranges, header fields, DTP modes. Spaced repetition is the most efficient way to do that: you review a fact just before you'd forget it, and the intervals grow as you learn it.

Anki is the best-known spaced repetition flashcard program.

  • Cost: free on Windows, macOS and Linux; AnkiDroid for Android is free and built by volunteers; the official iPhone app (AnkiMobile) is paid.
  • Pros: a strong algorithm, free sync across devices, works for any subject.
  • Cons: it starts empty, so you write the cards. The best cards are the ones you write yourself from your own mistakes anyway.

The Official Cert Guide also comes with flashcards if you already have it.

Practice apps

Google Play and the App Store have plenty of CCNA question apps, and their quality varies a lot. Before you pay, check three things: whether answers come with explanations (not just the correct option), whether the questions look original or lifted from dumps, and when the app was last updated.

What to avoid: exam dumps

"Dumps" are sets of real exam questions, recalled by candidates and then sold or posted online. They look like a shortcut, but:

  • they're prohibited. Cisco's exam policy says exam content is confidential and may not be copied or distributed. Penalties for violations can go as far as a lifetime ban from future Cisco exams and the nullification of every certification you already hold;
  • they're often wrong, and Cisco replaces questions once it learns they've leaked;
  • they don't teach networking. Your first technical interview or real outage will show it.

If a site promises "real exam questions", close the tab.

Putting it together by budget

Zero budget: the exam topics + the free Networking Academy courses (or your school's CCNA program) + Packet Tracer + Anki + the Cisco Learning Network for questions. You only pay for the exam (300 USD plus taxes, per Cisco's exam page).

Small budget: all of the above + one complete theory source (the Official Cert Guide) + a daily practice app such as Routerel.

Medium budget: all of the above + Boson ExSim-Max in the last 3–4 weeks as a final check.

Large budget or employer-funded: Cisco U. or an instructor-led course from a Cisco Learning Partner, plus practice exams.

Whatever mix you pick, the rule doesn't change: a little every day, a lab every week, and the exam topics checked off to the end. For a 12-week plan, see our guide to the CCNA, and for the math side, subnetting step by step.

Frequently asked questions

Can I prepare for the CCNA using only free resources?

Yes. The official exam topics, Packet Tracer, the Cisco Learning Network, the free Networking Academy courses and Anki cost nothing; you only pay for the exam. What you'll miss is one complete, structured theory source and high-quality practice exams, so you need more discipline.

Which book is best for the CCNA?

The best known is the CCNA 200-301 Official Cert Guide from Cisco Press, in two volumes, second edition from 2024. It covers v1.1. If you'll test after February 3, 2027 (v2.0), check whether an updated edition or digital update is available.

Is Packet Tracer enough for the CCNA?

For most CCNA-level labs, yes. It's free with a Networking Academy account and covers VLANs, routing, OSPF, ACLs, NAT, DHCP and much more. As a simulator it doesn't support every IOS command; for real Cisco images, move to Cisco Modeling Labs, GNS3 or EVE-NG.

How much does Boson ExSim-Max for CCNA cost?

On Boson's site, 99 USD for a one-year subscription with auto-renewal. It includes five practice exams with 370 questions in total.

Are exam dumps a good idea?

No. They're leaked exam questions shared without Cisco's permission. Cisco's exam policy forbids copying or distributing exam content, and penalties can go as far as a lifetime ban and the loss of every certification you hold.

Is Routerel a complete CCNA course?

No. It's an Android app with practice questions in English and Romanian, organized by CCNA 1, 2 and 3, with spaced repetition, a subnetting trainer and an exam simulation. It doesn't teach the theory from scratch and it isn't a lab simulator, so use it alongside a course or book and Packet Tracer.
