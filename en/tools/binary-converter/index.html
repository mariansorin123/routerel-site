Binary / decimal / hex converter

Type a number in decimal, binary or hexadecimal: an octet, a whole IPv4 address or a 32-bit number. See it in all three bases, with the place-value table and the working step by step.

The tool

How the conversion works, in short

  1. An octet has 8 bits, worth 128, 64, 32, 16, 8, 4, 2 and 1 from left to right.
  2. Decimal to binary: subtract the largest value that fits, one at a time. 77 = 64 + 8 + 4 + 1, so 01001101.
  3. Binary to decimal: add the values of the positions that hold a 1. 11000000 = 128 + 64 = 192.
  4. Binary to hex: split the octet into two groups of 4 bits and write each group as one hex digit. 0100 1101 = 4D.

The binary you need for subnetting is also covered in the guide Subnetting explained step by step.

Frequently asked questions

What can this converter handle?

A single octet (0–255), a whole IPv4 address written in decimal, binary or hex, and any 32-bit number (for example 3232235777 or 0xC0A80101). The result is shown in all three bases.

How do I convert decimal to binary quickly?

Start at 128 and subtract 128, 64, 32, 16, 8, 4, 2 and 1 in turn whenever they fit. Write 1 where you subtracted and 0 where you did not. For example 200 = 128 + 64 + 8, which is 11001000.

Why does binary matter for the CCNA?

Masks, network addresses and broadcasts only really make sense in binary: the network/host boundary is a number of bits. In the exam you convert by hand, so practise until it becomes automatic.

What does one hex digit represent?

One hex digit (0–9 and A–F) is exactly 4 bits, from 0000 to 1111. That is why an octet takes two hex digits, and why IPv6 and MAC addresses are written in hex.

Are the numbers I type sent anywhere?

No. Everything is converted in your browser, and nothing leaves your device.
